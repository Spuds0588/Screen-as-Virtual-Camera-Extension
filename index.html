Present in any meeting, without host rights.

Screen as Virtual Camera is a free Chrome extension that turns a tab, window, or screen into a camera your meeting can select. If you are allowed to turn your camera on, you can present: no host rights, no drivers, no desktop app.

Most meetings only let the host share

Screen sharing is usually reserved for the host, a co-host, or whoever the organization trusts. Guests, external presenters, and locked-down laptops get one lever: their camera. This extension turns a window, tab, or whole screen into that camera, so your demo arrives as your video feed instead of a share request somebody has to approve.

No host rights needed

You never ask for the screen-share slot. The meeting sees an ordinary camera device, and camera access belongs to every participant.

Nothing to install on the OS

It runs inside Chrome. There is no virtual camera driver, no bridge app, and no administrator rights required to start presenting.

Your layout stays intact

Slides and demos arrive as one clean 1280×720 frame in your video tile, not as a screen-share takeover of everyone else's meeting window.

How it works

Three steps, all inside the browser tab where the meeting is already running.

01

Start a capture

Open the toolbar popup and choose Share This Tab or Share Screen / Window, or press Ctrl+Shift+Y to capture the current tab. A webcam bubble can be composited into the corner.

02

Pick the virtual camera

In the meeting's camera menu choose Screen / Tab + Cam (Virtual). Sites see it as a normal camera device, so their own preview, mute, and device switching keep working.

03

Present, then stop

The feed stays live at 1280×720 and 30 fps while you present. Stop from the popup and the meeting shows a neutral card instead of a frozen last frame.

Private by design

Capture stays inside Chrome. Nothing is uploaded anywhere except the meeting you joined.

  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics, and no server of ours.
  • Frames travel from the extension's offscreen document to the meeting tab over a loopback WebRTC connection inside the browser.
  • Only capture permissions are requested — tabCapture, desktopCapture, offscreen, scripting, storage, activeTab — with no broad host access.
  • MIT licensed and small enough to read end to end.

What it does not do

  • It does not record your meeting or your screen.
  • It does not capture or inject audio.
  • It does not bypass meeting permissions — you still need camera access.
  • It cannot run where browser extensions are blocked by policy.

Questions presenters ask

Can I present in a meeting if I am not the host?

Yes, as long as the meeting lets you turn your camera on. The extension makes your captured tab, window, or screen appear as a camera, and camera access normally belongs to every participant. You never request the meeting's screen-share slot, so host and co-host controls do not apply.

Which meeting platforms does it work with?

Live calls are verified on Google Meet and Jitsi, and a live Webex guest meeting ran on the virtual camera. Zoom Web, Teams Web, and Whereby load the extension and expose the virtual camera on their join pages, but a live call is still pending on those three. That is why it is in beta.

Is it available in the Chrome Web Store?

Not yet. The beta is distributed from GitHub and installed as an unpacked extension. It will be listed in the Chrome Web Store once every primary platform has passed a live call.

How do I install the beta?

Download or clone the repository, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, choose Load unpacked, and select the extension folder. Then open your meeting and pick Screen / Tab + Cam (Virtual) in its camera menu. Ctrl+Shift+Y starts or stops a capture of the current tab.

Do I need a virtual camera driver or a desktop app?

No. Everything runs inside Chrome, so there is no OS-level virtual camera driver, no bridge process, and no admin install. It works in the browser version of the meeting, which is what Meet, Zoom Web, Jitsi, Webex, Teams Web, and Whereby load.

Does my real webcam still work?

Yes. A webcam picture-in-picture overlay can be composited into a corner of the shared feed, so people still see you. If you choose a different camera device in the meeting's own camera menu, the meeting gets your real webcam instead of the virtual camera.

Why does my own video look mirrored?

Meet, Jitsi, and Zoom mirror your self-view with CSS, so text reads backwards in your own window while everyone else sees it the right way round. The extension also has a Mirror output toggle for people who want their own preview readable, but it reverses the feed for everyone else, so it is off by default.

What happens to meeting audio?

Audio keeps coming from your normal microphone; the extension only replaces the camera. Capturing the shared tab's audio is not done on purpose, because playing it back into the meeting causes echo and feedback.

Does my screen or capture get uploaded anywhere?

No. Capture and compositing happen inside Chrome, and frames travel to the meeting tab through a loopback WebRTC connection in the browser. There is no account, no analytics, and no server of ours involved.
